5 signs your home is vulnerable in an earthquake

Urmia lies in a seismic region. You can spot these five signs yourself. If your home has two of them, it is time for an engineer's visit.

1. Built before the 1990s

Most houses built before Iran's seismic code (Standard 2800) was seriously enforced were not designed for earthquake forces. Find the year of construction on the title deed or completion certificate.

2. Brick walls without tie beams

Horizontal and vertical concrete tie beams stitch brick walls together. Without them, walls separate in an earthquake and the roof loses its support.

3. Jack-arch floors without ties

In a jack-arch floor, bricks arch between steel beams. If the beams are not tied together with rods or straps, they spread apart in an earthquake and the bricks fall.

4. Open ground floor with slender columns

A parking level or shop with only a few columns and no walls creates a "soft storey". In an earthquake, most of the deformation happens there.

5. Added storey or diagonal cracks

A floor added later without calculations puts unplanned loads on the walls and foundations. Diagonal cracks next to doors and windows are usually a sign of settlement or shear weakness.

This list does not replace an engineer's visit. Its purpose is to help you know when to ask for one.
