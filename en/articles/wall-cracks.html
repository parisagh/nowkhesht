Wall cracks: when should you worry?

Not every crack is dangerous. This guide helps you tell a surface crack from one that should be checked.

Cracks that are usually superficial

  • Fine, irregular hairline cracks in plaster and paint, often from shrinkage of the render.
  • Small cracks where two different materials meet, for example a wall and a suspended ceiling.

Cracks that should be checked

  • Diagonal cracks from the corner of a door or window; often a sign of settlement or shear weakness.
  • A crack that is wide, or whose two sides are not flush.
  • A crack that gets longer or wider over time.
  • A crack together with sticking doors and windows or a sloping floor.

How to monitor a crack

Mark both ends of the crack with a pencil and write the date. Take a photo with a ruler next to it. Look again a few weeks later; if it has passed the mark or widened, it is time for a visit.

After every earthquake, even a small one, record new cracks the same way.
